## Owner decision 2026-10-03: six layers with locality re-placement

A cut-line bound (`scripts/pcbgen/cut_capacity.py`) showed that the four-layer halves could not carry their signal nets with intact ground and power planes. About 400 nets had to cross single jack-pin columns whose front/back capacity was only 0.80–0.86 of that demand. The owner chose both remedies: six copper layers and locality re-placement of the free surface-mount packages. Panel hardware, LEDs and headers do not move.

- **Placement:** `scripts/checks/jack_locality.py` replaces the connectivity-blind first-fit J placement. Each free package is pulled toward the fixed jacks, LEDs and headers on its signal nets and toward its own module's fixed hardware, then legalized onto the nearest free courtyard cells. IC/bypass clusters are placed first and keep their supply-pad alignment (worst 2.8875 mm against the 3 mm proposal). After legalization, packages with the same footprint trade slots (side and rotation travel with the slot, so every swap stays legal) to cut span-weighted wire length, with a quadratic penalty above 60 mm. This cuts the estimated wire length from 25.5 to 18.1 m on JL (nets longer than 60 mm 79 → 21) and from 25.1 to 18.0 m on JR (53 → 13). The output is byte-stable across hash seeds.
- **Stack:** F.Cu, In2.Cu, In3.Cu and B.Cu carry signals; In1.Cu is a continuous AGND plane; In3.Cu also carries the −12 V fill; In4.Cu is a continuous +12 V plane. +5 V is routed as 0.4 mm tracks. Signals on In3.Cu are accepted only if the −12 V fill stays connected, and native checks reject any batch that splits it.
- **Neck-down at IC pin rows:** `scripts/pcbgen/neckdown_areas.py` draws a rule area (nothing disallowed) over every IC whose pins carried an open signal net on the routed drafts, covering its pads plus 2 mm, from `design/partition/neckdown-areas.json`. Each half's `.kicad_dru` lets Default-class signal copper inside these areas use 0.15 mm track width and 0.15 mm signal-to-signal clearance (0.2 mm / 0.2 mm elsewhere). This is common practice and within standard six-layer fabrication capability; it gives the router room to escape the quad op-amp pin rows. Rails and AGND keep their 0.25 mm net-class clearance, and the grid router applies the same rule per region.
- **Evidence chain:** `scripts/checks/jack_locality.py` proves the partition changed only in jack package faces, jack bypass distances and the jack layer stack, and chains onto the earlier connector-locality transition. Superseded four-layer stage reports were removed; git history keeps them.

Routing uses `scripts/pcbgen/route_jack_grid.py`: terminal via arrays, ±12 V plane fanouts, rail links, then a negotiated-congestion (PathFinder) pass over every signal net on a 0.05 mm grid (region stages re-run on a 0.035 mm grid inside the neck-down areas), rip-up-and-reroute batches for what it leaves open, with AGND stitching vias where new copper cuts a surface pour. Each stage is applied to a disposable copy, checked with native DRC, schematic parity and zone refill, and kept only when the native open-edge count falls. Rip-up-and-reroute re-offers still-open islands with 4, 8, 12 and then 20 victim nets per probe, and a stranded island joins whichever island of its net is cheapest to reach, not only the largest. Stage receipts are in `boards/<board-id>/reports/grid-routing/`; `reports/ratsnest.json` holds the native per-net topology after a single zone refill; the open-edge figures above come from DRC with refilled zones, which can be higher. Silkscreen/text warnings remain [#44](https://github.com/Takazudo/zudo-osc-hole-field/issues/44).
