The OPA388IDBVR study asks whether an inverting amplifier could replace the negative monitor's external-reference dependency. It produces a conditional DC error allowance and a passive port screen. It does **not** select a replacement or change the captured monitor. Issue #59 remains open; circuit, timing, current, return-integrity and signal-isolation acceptance remain unproved.

The retained source is Texas Instruments SBOS777D, revised July 2020, with a 15 July 2026 package addendum. The exact order entry is OPA388IDBVR, five-pin DBV SOT-23, 3000-piece large tape-and-reel. Its Active/Production entry is not a stock or assembly-allocation claim. The [manufacturer datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa388.pdf) supplies pin functions, ratings and conditioned characteristics; cached bytes and SHA-256 are recorded in `design/power/negative-inverter-candidate-sources.json`.

## Candidate connectivity

| Element | Unselected connection |
|---|---|
| OPA388IDBVR pins 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | NEG_MAG, AGND, AGND, NEG_SUM, +5 V |
| RN1, RT0603BRD07150KL, 150 kΩ | VN to RN_MID |
| RN2, RT0603BRD071KL, 1 kΩ | RN_MID to NEG_SUM |
| RF, RT0603BRD074K99L, 4.99 kΩ | NEG_MAG to NEG_SUM |
| RG, RT0603BRD07150RL, 150 Ω | NEG_SUM to AGND |
| Hypothetical monitor interface | NEG_MAG to existing U101 SENSE2 pin 3 |

U101 is the existing TPS37044MJOFDDFRQ1. Its spare SENSE2 input uses a 0.4 V, ±7% window and shares RESET1_N with SENSE1. If this topology were eventually selected, the old reference/comparator network would be removed and SENSE1 left unused according to the manufacturer's instruction to leave unused SENSE inputs floating. That is a proposed interface change, not a committed rewire. The captured U101 still has SENSE2 unused and SENSE1 connected to REF_SENSE.

No bypass choice, compensation network, PCB placement or native schematic is added for the OPA388. The missing supply and output parasitics matter to startup, collapse and stability.

## Static allowance, not a device error guarantee

The calculation keeps the current negative-rail normal magnitude of 11.74–12.37 V. Exact resistor sheets provide ±0.1% initial tolerance and ±25 ppm/°C TCR. It applies independent multiplicative tolerance and temperature factors over −40…125 °C, with the existing **assumed** 25 °C resistance reference; that reference temperature is not stated by those sheets.

The ideal gain range is approximately 0.03281584–0.03327850. The normal output spans 0.38525796–0.41165498 V, inside the conservative common static recovery window, 0.384377184–0.413992416 V. The strict uniform allowance for **all additive output error together** is approximately **0.880783 mV**. Equality loses strict recovery margin; the report retains an exact fraction and rounds the displayed allowance inward.

RG increases the noise gain to as much as 34.53363. If offset were the only error, the input allowance would be 25.5050 µV. If inverting-input current were the only error, its allowance would be 0.175893 µA. These separate allowances cannot each be consumed in full at once.

The OPA388's full-temperature 7.5 µV offset row would contribute about 0.259003 mV at that noise gain **if its conditions applied**. Its 700 pA bias row would contribute about 3.506 µV through RF. Neither is an actual application bound:

- Offset common conditions are VCM = VOUT = VS/2 and 10 kΩ connected to VS/2. The candidate operates near ground common-mode with approximately 0.4 V output.
- Full-temperature CMRR minima are at VS = 2.5 V and 5.5 V, with different common-mode ranges. The 2.5 V row excludes the negative rail itself; the 5.5 V row extends 50 mV below it. The candidate's 4.81–5.2 V supply is not silently interpolated between these rows.
- The bias row specifies RIN = 100 kΩ. The grounded 150 Ω shunt changes the actual source impedance. The AOL/load and output-swing rows also retain their stated load references. The candidate ideally sources up to 82.209 µA through RF, while a 10 kΩ load to mid-supply at this low output would require approximately 199–222 µA of sinking. A lighter current of the opposite polarity does not establish table-load equivalence.
- Supply rejection, common-mode rejection, finite gain, output load, noise and transient errors need a joint application bound. No sum of unrelated table cross-sections is promoted to that bound.

## Fault and power-state limits

Under the **additional hypothetical assumption** that the same total-error allowance holds at VN = −10.9 V, the output is below every UV trip corner. The minimum overdrive is only about **0.6643%**, far below the TPS table's 10% condition. Its 10 µs maximum also requires valid supply, a fixed reset delay greater than 1 ms, 10 kΩ pullup and 10 pF output load. The real shared reset loading is not established here. Even greater overdrive does not authorize an assumed monotonic timing guarantee.

Normal-band compatibility is not fault coverage. At nominal resistances and zero amplifier error, VN = −12.48 V produces about 0.412419 V, between the nominal UV/OV trip thresholds of 0.372/0.428 V. A previously released negative-channel reset need not trip, even though that rail is outside the normal band. This prescribed DC countermodel is not a measured trajectory or a new accepted fault limit.

The OPA388 slew, settling and overload-recovery entries are typical. The 2 µs settling entry is at gain one, a 1 V step and ±2.5 V supply; it is not a maximum for this high-noise-gain inverter. TPS nominal startup is likewise not a maximum. No total fault, startup or release time is calculated.

The passive screen prescribes VN within ±12.48 V, output within 0–5.2 V, and an independent ±10 µA sensitivity current at NEG_SUM. The output driver may be external, retained charge or a hypothetical semiconductor state. Including RF backfeed gives:

| Prescribed-port result | Conditional bound |
|---|---|
| NEG_SUM with RG = 150 Ω | −13.569 mV to +166.203 mV |
| Current through RF | Approximately −2.729 µA to +1.018 mA |
| RF dissipation | Below 5.155 mW in this box |
| Largest RN1 dissipation | Below 1.052 mW in this box |

The resistor currents do **not** bound amplifier current into a forced or shorted output. With VN negative and OUT clamped low, the amplifier can drive against that clamp. The datasheet short-circuit entries are typical (60 mA at 5.5 V, 30 mA at 2.5 V), not maximum current guarantees or proof of the auxiliary budget.

At zero amplifier supply the differential-input stress rating is 0.2 V. The 150 Ω screen stays inside it; the otherwise identical **1 kΩ shunt countermodel exceeds it**. This is a computable defect of that weaker passive argument, not evidence of measured device failure.

For a separate cold high-impedance output model, two independent ±10 µA sensitivities represent summing-node and output/TPS currents. The output range is approximately ±65.555 mV. These currents are deliberately variable sensitivities, **not manufacturer leakage maxima**. A negative output within the TPS −0.3 V absolute limit is still outside its 0 V minimum recommended SENSE voltage. An output below −0.3 V directly conflicts with the TPS stress rating; RG does not isolate that output pin.

The enumerated passive boxes do not establish actual cold output clamps, partial-supply behavior, rail-order trajectories, saturation recovery, retained-capacitor injection or output backdrive limits. Reversed VN would demand a negative linear output and invokes unqualified saturation behavior. Local AGND loss invalidates the coordinate reference used by every calculation and remains independently unresolved.

Resistor power bounds use maximum voltage-drop magnitude squared divided by minimum resistance; they do not assume power is extremal at resistor corners. The exact sheets rate 0.1 W at 70 °C and state a 75 V maximum continuous voltage at full rated power. Full-temperature derating and installed temperatures are not proved. The OPA388 DBV thermal metric is a test-board characterization, not an installed thermal bound.

## Conditional current comparison

A hypothetical replacement removes the old reference output demand, reference IQ and dual-comparator IQ once, while keeping the other conservative monitor branches. Adding the OPA388's **5.5 V, unloaded** 2.6 mA IQ row, ideal feedback demand and the TPS input-current row gives approximately 4.671827 mA on +5 V and 82.209 µA on the negative rail; the old +12 V screen remains unchanged.

This comparison explicitly borrows table values outside their full conditions. TPS adjustable-input current is tabulated at SENSE = 5.5 V, not the candidate's 0.4 V. Actual OPA current at 4.81–5.2 V, feedback drive, input/output leakage, bypass charging, compensation, partial-power injection and dynamic current remain unqualified. The original 20 mA auxiliary allocations do not change. The current monitor report and its scalar values are preserved.

## Reproduction and remaining work

Run `python3 scripts/checks/negative_inverter_candidate.py --check` for the committed report and `--verify-retained` when the ignored PDF cache is present. Portable tests exercise strict boundaries, KCL, resistor interiors, cold-current sensitivities, the rejected 1 kΩ countermodel, source/condition mutations and frozen-input provenance. Canonical regeneration regenerates the study after the current monitor report.

A next circuit selection requires an actual joint error/load envelope and protected output/power-state contract, plus supported fault timing and compensation. The present study makes those requirements quantitative without replacing the captured monitor. Native capture, dynamic IC simulation and bench qualification are **NOT RUN** for this unselected topology. Return integrity and the instrument's signal-disconnect obligations remain separate open gates.
