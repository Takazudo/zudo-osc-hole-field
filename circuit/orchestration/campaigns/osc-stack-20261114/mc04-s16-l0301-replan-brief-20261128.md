## What this study answers

Can the signals of P-K-P-L03.01 be carried by a different set of stock links, so that no connector, slot or tail
stands on U8409, while every protected part stays where DS-150 has it, the circuit and the parts stay as they are,
and every other link stays at its place?

It answers on the link gate's own rules and on courtyard-level geometry. It does not regenerate the gate, and it
says nothing electrical.

## What S12 to S15 did not try

S12 and S15 moved connectors. Both kept every link's signals and member sites. S16 changes which link carries
which signal, and holds everything else, DS-150 included.

## Design-authority rulings made in this brief

All are provisional until the independent review accepts them.

### Ruling P1: a re-plan is a ride set and a partition, by site

A **structure** is:

- a set R of member sites whose signals **ride** on other live links of joint P-K with front board P, by the rule
  of way 1 with the cap (`ride_capped`, Ruling P7): each signal goes to the nearest host within its class's rider
  distance whose order still fits its own size; and
- a partition of the remaining sites into **blocks**. Each block becomes one stock link at the smallest of 8, 14
  and 26 circuits that holds its order under Ruling P7, with the cable the options file gives that size, and its
  search centre at the centroid of its sites, as the gate computes it.

A site either rides whole or sits whole in one block. The signals of a riding site may go to different hosts.

**Site order.** Way 1 fills hosts as it goes, so the result depends on the order of the sites. The ride test
tries every order of the sites of R (at most 720), with each site's signals in site order. A ride set is illegal
only if every order fails. The scene uses the first legal order, taking orders in lexicographic order of site id.
The number of distinct host plans over all legal orders is recorded. Hosts keep their place, so the choice of plan
changes no geometry and no replay row.

There are 877 structures (64 ride sets times the partitions of the rest). One of them is the present plan. The
other 876 are the domain.

**Five exclusions, stated in the report with the verdict:**

| # | Outside the domain | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | the 40-way size, which is stock on this joint | the six sites fit 26 circuits; a larger size is growth |
| b | a block of the same structure as a host for a rider | hosts are links at their record |
| c | a ride that divides a site | the record has one (`/E1/STAGE_FALL` of K-P-19, DS-121, for coupling); a study by site does not search them |
| d | a slot more than 35 mm from its block centre | S12 Ruling 1 set 35 mm for eight named links; this study applies the same radius to new links |
| e | a block slot with a member site beyond its class distance of the connector centres | R2 applies it to every new slot, as S9 and S12 did; the gate applies it only to a place found by way 2, and 13 recorded links do not meet it (Ruling P8) |

A size larger than needed is also outside the domain.

### Ruling P7: the logic-run cap of DS-120 and DS-122 is part of every allocation

A re-plan is the clustering running again. DS-122 rules that case: a run of n logic lines needs ⌈n / 3⌉ − 1
grounds inside it. DS-120 gives the cap of three and says the runs are as equal as they can be. `allocation`,
`allocation_errors` and `circuits_needed` do not know this rule. The generator applies it in `links_gate_c6.py`,
a later pass that acts on eight links by name, L03.01 among them.

- **The order builder.** `capped_order(signals, size)` takes the sequence that `links_gate.allocation` gives for
  the signal list on 60 positions and drops the unused positions. Each run of n > 3 logic lines becomes ⌈n / 3⌉
  runs, as equal as possible, the longer first, in the same signal order, with one ground between runs. The
  sequence is padded to the size with unused positions. If it is longer than the size, there is no order.
- **Signal order.** A block's list is its member sites in id order, each site's signals in site order, as
  `link_record` builds it. A host's list (`host_signals`) is the signals on the host's recorded positions: first those of
  its member sites in that order, then its recorded riders in record order. New riders follow in ride order. A
  line that rode out of the host is not on its positions and is not in the list.
- **The check.** `links_gate_c6.order_errors` on the built order, with no `original`, must return nothing. It
  covers `allocation_errors`, the cap, and the ground on each side of a SYNC_GATE line. The builder does not place
  SYNC_GATE grounds, so a link with such a line fails the check and is neither a block nor a host. None of the six
  sites has one, and the nearest link with one is 148.4 mm from the nearest of the six sites.
- **Size.** A block's size is the smallest of 8, 14 and 26 at which the builder gives an order. For all 63 site
  subsets that equals `circuits_needed` plus ⌈n / 3⌉ − 1 for n ≥ 4 logic lines.
- **Hosts.** A link is eligible as a host only if the builder reproduces its recorded order: `capped_order` on
  `host_signals` at its own size equals its recorded positions. A link that is not eligible refuses every rider
  and is listed with the full hosts in a reason text. 10 of the 39 other P-K links on board P are not eligible
  (ruled c6 orders with single lines, SYNC_GATE lines, rider order). An eligible host accepts a rider only if the builder gives an order for its list with the rider at the host's
  own size, with no error. A host that takes a rider gets the builder's order. Every other link keeps its
  recorded order.
- **What the builder is checked against.** It reproduces the recorded positions of P-K-P-L03.01 and
  P-K-P-L01.02.01, the two links the c6 pass splits by this rule, and of P-K-P-L05.01.01, P-K-P-L03.02.01 and
  P-K-P-L01.01.02 (control C3b).
- **What stays `NOT RUN`.** DS-122's stop test, the pair ratio at or below 1.0 by the pair function of ST-04, is
  not run: it belongs to the c6 pass on a generated record. A built order meets the cap by count. It is not a
  ruled order.

### Ruling P8: the record slot is exempt from the class-distance clause of R2

The run on Revision 3 found that R2 rejects L03.01's own record slot: P-K-E3 is 52.075 mm from the connector
centres, against 45 mm for ANALOGUE. The executor applied no reading and stopped, which was right.

- **The gate does not break its own rule.** The generator applies the class distance only to a place found by
  way 2 (`ways_out`, `check_ways`; DS-84). An ordinary place is bounded by the search around the cluster centre.
  L03.01 has an ordinary place and no way-out entry. Its record is consistent, and no gate check fails on it.
- **The studies' rule is stricter than the record.** S9, S12, S15 and this study apply the class distance to every
  moved or new slot (S12 Ruling 6, DS-158: "class distances … unchanged"). At their record slots, 13 of the 59
  placed links outside joint O-K have a site beyond it, L03.01 among them; the largest excess is 12.064 mm on
  JR-K-JR-L01. None was placed by way 2. These are read-only figures from the S12 `World`.
- **Ruling.** The record slot of a link is exempt from the class-distance clause of R2, as it is from R4 to R6.
  The cable and compliance clauses of R2 are still evaluated there; L03.01's record passes both. R2 is unchanged
  for every block slot.
- **Scope.** The exemption touches the present plan only: C3, C10 and M1. The present plan is outside the domain
  and fails A0, so no verdict can rest on it.
- **Beyond this study.** Nothing in the record needs correcting. What the figure does show is that S12 and S15
  judged moved slots of these 13 links by a limit their records do not meet. For L03.01, rule 2 removed 630 of
  813 legal slots in S12; how many of those fail only this clause was not measured. Whether a slot "no farther
  than the record" would be acceptable is an electrical question, and nothing on record answers it. This study
  does not relax the clause. It labels it as exclusion e.

### Ruling P9: controls on P-K-P-L13.01.01.01 use its record block

C5, M9, M10 and M12 exercise the census on a link with known survivors. The brief called it "the three sites at 8
circuits". Those sites carry 4 LOGIC and 2 ANALOGUE lines, which need 9 positions under Ruling P7. The recorded
link fits 8 only because `/E1/STAGE_FALL` of K-P-19 rode out for coupling (DS-121), which is exclusion c.

- **Ruling.** These four cases use the **record block** of the link, `record_block(lid)`: the recorded link with
  its members, centre, size and cable, and the signals on its recorded positions (`host_signals`, five lines).
  S1 and S2 are evaluated on that list: the builder gives an order on 7 of the 8 positions with no error.
- **Scope.** A record block is used in controls and mutations only. Stages 1 to 2b build blocks from whole sites
  by `block_record`, so the domain and exclusion c are unchanged. Stage 4 moves the recorded link as S12 did and
  builds no block.
- C5 already passed on Revision 3, because the census runs R1 to R6 only.

### Stage 1: block census (complete, no cap)

For each of the 62 site subsets other than the full set: `census_block` at the block's size (Ruling P7) on the
three grids within 35 mm, with every live link except L03.01 placed at its record. Rules R1 to R6 in order.
Candidates are reduced to distinct (slot, direction) pairs with the finest grid kept. Counts after each rule are
recorded per block, with the ring of each survivor: up to 24 mm, or 24 to 35 mm, as S12 reported it.

The full set is also censused, as a reference only. It is the present plan's link, which is outside the domain.
Its census shows whether one 26-way link of all six sites has any other slot under S16's rules, and no verdict
uses it.

### Stage 2a: structure screen (complete, no replay)

For each of the 876 structures: S1 and S2 for its blocks, S3 over every order of the sites of R, then "every
block has at least one census survivor". The result is the list of **live** structures, with the reason for each
dead one, the first legal site order and the number of distinct host plans.

### Stage 2b: joint search per live structure

**C12 first.** The first 12 complete legal scenes of stage 2b are replayed in sequence and again through the
pool, and the row digests must be equal. With fewer than 12 scenes, all of them are used. With none, C12 is
`NOT RUN` (no replay exists), and no pool result is used anywhere. A difference is STOP 1. The sequential replays
count in this stage's time and not in the cap of 1200.

Live structures are taken in this order: fewest links, then fewest circuits, then lowest summed cost of the
blocks' cheapest survivors. For each, blocks take slots from their pools (cheapest survivor per spot, a spot being
a direction and a 2.0 mm cell, at most 20 per block), best-first with `legal_block`. Each complete legal scene
gets one rows-only replay and is judged by A0, A1, A2, A3, A4 and A6. A structure stops at its first passing scene.

### Stage 3: overlay items on the kept scenes

The 5 best passing scenes (ranking below) are judged by `judge_replan`: A5 and A7 to A11, with the S8 block
search where A8 calls for it.

### Stage 4: what each kept scene means for U5210

For each scene that passes stage 3: the 23 distinct S12 survivors of P-K-P-L13.01.01.01 are tested for legality
with the re-planned links in place, in S12's recorded order. Each legal one gets one rows-only replay, judged by A0 to A4
and A6 and by "U5210 has no row". The cap is 115 replays (5 scenes times 23), so it cannot be reached. The result
is a reading, `U5210 FOLLOW-ON SEEN AT ROWS LEVEL` or `NOT SEEN`, never a verdict.

### Ranking

1. Fewest links. 2. Fewest displaced K footprints. 3. Fewest parts with leads to cut. 4. Fewest protected
footprints newly under a tail (S12 Ruling 3). 5. Lowest summed cost.

## STOP conditions

Stop, write what was found, and return to the design authority.

1. Any control fails, or an input, tree, code or source pin differs.
2. `regen-all.sh` at the start leaves a tracked change, or the pinned KiCad oracle cannot be provided (DS-196
   governs a daemon stopped by a reboot).
3. The computed vector of a mutation, or a reason text given with it, differs from the expected one.
4. The machinery cannot supply something a ruling assumes: a block record for a site subset, a built order that
   `order_errors` accepts, a ride through the derived function, a replay with a changed link set. No substitute
   may be invented.
5. Stage 1 or stage 2a cannot complete inside its ceiling.
6. The cumulative ceiling, a step ceiling, the memory guard, or a fourth resume.
7. An ambiguity in a rule. Take the stricter reading only if it cannot change the verdict, record it, and
   continue; otherwise stop.
8. Any step would write outside the study directory, the cache, the durable copy, the report, and the temporary
   snapshot and scratch roots that `rp.snapshot_root` and S12's full replay create in the system temporary
   directory, outside the repository. Or it would touch a sibling repository, or need an outward action.

A result with no re-plan is a valid result, not a STOP.
