## Verdict: flex is an acceptable way to go, at the price of regrouping 513 signals and cutting about 49 slots in K

A flexible joint removes the hand soldering of 191 of the 197 stackers, the alignment problem, the mating force and every aid that reviews A1 and A2 added for them. It does not drop into the present footprints. The plan's premise (a header pair replaces a harness connector at its own site, with no neighbour moved) ends for the K joints: the 191 sites are replaced by about 49 connector fields on each side, every one of the 513 interface signals is re-routed on two boards, and K gets one slot per link. The plan page had rejected this class for exactly that reason ("every interface signal would be re-routed"). The reason still holds; what changed is the other side of the comparison, which reviews A1 and A2 made far more expensive than the plan assumed, and the owner's direction (DS-35, DS-37).

The decision is close on rework and clear on hand work and risk. It is decided for flex, with a paper gate that can still send the design back to the rigid stack.

### The premise it ends, stated plainly

- **Every site moves.** Today each pair stands where a harness connector stood, inside that connector's courtyard, and the nets on both boards end there. In the flex design 191 sites on K and 191 on the front boards are deleted and about 49 fields per side are placed anew. Every one of the 513 signals is re-routed on its front board and on K, on average 11 to 13 mm and at most about 29 mm before the fan-in to 1 mm pitch. The ground positions (264 on K today) are re-allocated.
- **K gets slots.** Both mating faces are hidden once K is on its spacers (front-board B.Cu and K F.Cu face each other across 13 to 19 mm). A latch cannot be worked in that gap. The working assumption is therefore: the front-board end is plugged with K off; each link passes through a slot in K; its K end is latched on K's rear face, which stays reachable. That is about 49 slots of about 24 × 1.5 mm, near 2 % of K's area (ASSUMED sizes), each cutting every layer of the densest board. The connector on K moves from F.Cu to B.Cu, among K's rear parts. Bringing every link around K's edge instead would avoid the slots and move all 513 signals to the board edges; it is refused as the larger rework. Slots in the front boards with the latches on their panel side would put every latch behind the panel, so that K could not come off without removing the panel; refused.
- **Signals run together.** Today each site carries two signals beside their own AGND position. A link carries up to 14 or so signals side by side over 40 to 60 mm of unshielded flat conductor. Coupling between neighbouring analogue signals and the return path of the precision nets are NOT RUN and no figure exists. A 2-layer flex with a ground layer is within the factory's range (SOURCED); a catalogue jumper has none.
- **Ground current exceeds the right-angle connector's rating as allocated today.** ST-04 computes 0.54 to 0.57 A in the worst AGND header contact in the normal case and up to 0.94 A with a branch's ground posts open (`design/stack/power-posts/README.md`, DERIVED, model ASSUMED). The 52271 right-angle connector is rated 0.5 A per contact (SOURCED); the vertical 52610 is rated 1.0 A. The regrouped links have spare positions (49 × 22 = 1,078 against 747), so more ground positions are available, but the share must be recomputed.
- **Thirty mating cycles.** The slider connectors are rated for at most 30 matings (SOURCED). That is enough for assembly and occasional service and is a hard limit on how often K comes off. The retained record of the rigid family states 500 cycles (`design/stack/connectors/README.md`).

### What it gives

- **No hand soldering of the K-joint connectors.** The documents do not support machine placement for 185 of the 197 stackers (DS-29); 191 stackers disappear. What remains by hand is six 1×5 adapter stackers (30 joints), if the assembler cannot place them.
- **No alignment problem.** The sourced limits of 0.07 and 0.16 mm final and 0.39 and 0.57 mm initial misalignment, and the computed 0.27 mm, concern rigid pairs. A flex link tolerates board-to-board offsets of millimetres. The registration plate, the pilot locators, the 40 mm rule, the 18 added fixing sites, the closing load, the draw-down and the jack-out all lose their reason (F2).
- **No mating force on the boards.** The stack mated about 750 N in one step (777 positions at about 0.97 N); a slider connector is latched one at a time.
- **K's plane, the gaps and the depth do not change.** The gaps were set by the panel hardware and the selector, not by the headers. The flex needs a bend radius of about 2.4 mm for a 0.2 mm 2-layer flex at 12 × (DERIVED from SOURCED figures), which a 17 or 19 mm gap holds. A right-angle connector on K's rear face is 3.00 mm high (SOURCED), inside the 5.0 mm rear part envelope; a vertical one at 5.75 mm is not. Depth stays about 40 mm.
- **The links carry no parts.** Both connectors sit on the rigid boards and are placed in their normal assembly. The link is a bare flex with stiffened tails, or a catalogue jumper, so the factory's flex assembly is not needed.
- **Handling, for a first use of flex.** A link is thin and tolerant of bending along its length, and it is damaged by a sharp crease, by pulling at an angle and by forcing a slider. It is inserted straight, the slider is closed with a fingernail or a plastic tool, and a damaged link is replaced, not repaired (description ASSUMED; the 30-cycle figure is SOURCED).
- **Service.** With K's rear face open: unlatch 49 sliders, remove the K-side screws, lift K off the slack links, which stay in the front boards. No force, no fixture.
- **Cost and channel.** Flex and its assembly are offered by the channel the project uses, at a published entry price of 2.00 USD for five small 2-layer pieces (SOURCED; not a quote, and 49 links in several shapes will cost more). Catalogue jumpers need no flex order at all, if length, circuit count and contact side exist as stock items (UNSOURCED for the lengths and counts needed). Connector prices and the factory's stock of the connector are UNSOURCED. Lead time is UNSOURCED.
