# DA-01: design review A1 and decisions D1 to D6

Campaign `osc-stack-20261114`, task DA-01, attempt a1, 2026-11-14. Start commit `d74bce019ea74e7be1da6eef7d37c30f6a31b01b` on `stack/da-01`. Author: the campaign's design authority. This file reviews ST-01 and ST-03 and records six decisions. It changes no design source. Every schematic, board and stack-up stays an unvalidated draft. Fit, force, alignment, strain and thermal checks are **NOT RUN**: no specimen exists.

Status words follow ST-01: **SOURCED** (a retained or newly fetched document states it, cited by file and locator), **DERIVED** (arithmetic on sourced values), **ASSUMED** (a working value with its closing check), **UNSOURCED LEAD** (remembered, not evidence).

## Summary: the stack continues as a changed design, and it is not adoptable before a coupon

- The recommended pair of ST-01 (SMM socket with TW-SM stacker) cannot give the three gaps to K. The socket accepts a post only between 3.05 and 3.25 mm deep, and the stacker's pin tip height is fixed by one of six lead styles. The 0.13 mm stacker-height step moves the insulator, not the pin tip.
- The retained MMS page and the stacker page give a working pair: the single-row MMS socket (4.50 mm, insertion 2.13 to 4.32 mm) with the same TW-SM stacker. It closes at an adapter gap `h` = 12.8 mm with 0.74 mm of Z margin. K's front face moves to z = −30.4 mm and the depth target to about 40 mm.
- Ten manufacturer documents fetched today source the alignment and force figures that ST-01 and ST-03 marked UNSOURCED. Allowed initial misalignment is 0.39 mm along the row and 0.57 mm across it. Allowed final misalignment is 0.07 mm along the row and 0.16 mm across it. Mating force is 0.71 to 0.97 N per position when new and up to 1.33 N after 100 cycles.
- The paper budget meets the initial figure on root sum of squares and misses the final along-row figure at any plausible placement accuracy. Only a measurement closes this. Gate A therefore cannot adopt the K joints before the coupon exists.
- ST-03's Z condition (a tie post within 15.5 to 23 mm of every pair) is not reachable: with every clear site used, 121 of 197 pairs stay farther than 15.5 mm. The MMS insertion window relaxes the condition to 40 mm, which five added sites meet.

## D1. The direct stack continues as a changed stack; it is not adopted on paper

**Decision.** The direct stack stays the working architecture for the rest of Phase 0, with six changes:

1. The joints to K use the MMS −02 −SV socket with the TW-SM stacker (D3).
2. The adapter gap is `h` = 12.8 mm. K's F.Cu plane is z = −30.4 mm. The depth target is about 40 mm (D3).
3. The stack closes with the front boards in the panel and their nuts loose; K is drawn on by jack screws; the nuts are tightened last (D2).
4. EL joins P on six double-row 2×4 pairs: a CLT socket on EL and a double-row TW-SM stacker on a wider P tab (D3).
5. Every pair lies within 40 mm of a post or tie post. Posts and tie posts double as jack-screw points (D4).
6. P is free in Z on its panel side (D4).

Gate A is not taken here. The K joints cannot be adopted before the coupon reports (D5).

**Reasons.**

- Heights now close on sourced catalogue figures, with one socket family and three stacker lead styles.
- Z closes on root sum of squares with a 1.48 mm sourced window, where the earlier pair had 0.20 mm.
- The capture (initial) limit is met on root sum of squares in the chosen order.
- Nothing in the evidence shows the concept failing. What it shows is one limit, 0.07 mm final misalignment along the row, that paper cannot meet. Whether the real pair tolerates the expected 0.1 to 0.25 mm is a property of the contact and of the free pin length, and it is measurable on a small coupon.
- The owner's intent is board-to-board joints. The alternatives below either keep the cables or restart K.

**Alternatives considered.**

| Alternative | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Continue as planned (SMM pair, `h` = 6.9 to 7.9 mm, stack first) | The pair cannot give the gaps (C1), fails ST-03's Z test (M6), and stack first leaves the hardware located by K |
| Stop now and keep the cable partition | The evidence does not say the concept fails; it says one limit is unproven. Stopping discards a closing height set and a closing Z budget before the one decisive measurement |
| Split K along the seam between the jack boards and P | Removes the two-plane strain case, which P free in Z already removes. It does not touch the per-pair alignment limit, needs a K-to-K joint with hundreds of contacts and discards the K placement |
| Floating board-to-board connectors | The class is multi-row, fine-pitch and fixed-height. It needs connector fields and re-routes every interface signal; no single-row 3-position floating part is known. UNSOURCED LEAD; a survey would be ST-01 scope if the coupon fails |
| Spring contacts on pads | Tolerant in XY and Z, but about 0.5 to 1 N of permanent preload per contact (400 to 800 N on K) and no retained part at 12.8 to 18.8 mm |
| Through-hole sockets soldered while mated | Removes the per-pair error, but the opposite face is occupied at 127 of 127 P sites, 56 of 56 J sites and 145 of 191 K sites |

**Evidence status.**

| Term | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Gap sets per socket and lead style | DERIVED from SOURCED catalogue and print values (`heights.py`) |
| Initial and final misalignment limits | SOURCED (specification revision A); the tables name the fixed-height terminal as the mate, and the specification's title covers the stacker. Applying them to the stacker is ASSUMED |
| Surface-mount float 0.1 mm per half, lean 0.5° | ASSUMED (ST-03); coupon C-2 and C-3 close them |
| Tolerance of the real pair to 0.1 to 0.25 mm of final offset | UNSOURCED; only coupon C-4 closes it |
| Force per position | SOURCED for the double-row pair; ASSUMED for the single-row pair; coupon C-1 |

**How to reverse.** Nothing adopted changes. To return to the plan page's stack, set the adapter height parameter back to 6.9 mm. To stop the stack, record Gate A negative; the cable partition is still the working design.

**Consequences.** Depth grows from about 34 to about 40 mm. The posts become 17.0 and 18.8 mm. ST-02 and ST-03 are re-run with new parameters. ST-01 gets an addendum. The coupon becomes the critical path to adoption. If coupon C-4 fails, the K joints of JL, JR and P fail with it, and the design authority then decides between the cable partition with K moved forward and a connector survey of the floating class.

Closing order:

1. Build the P unit: seat EL on P against the backing platen.
2. Mate the three adapters onto K and tighten their standoff screws.
3. Lay the panel face down. Fit JL, JR and the P unit. Fit every jack and toggle nut finger-loose.
4. Lower K onto the pilot pins. Start every post and tie-post jack screw by hand.
5. Draw K down in quarter turns until the posts seat. Tighten the post and tie-post screws.
6. Tighten the jack nuts, then the toggle nuts on their take-up, then the selector bushing nuts.

Rear service removes K by the same jack screws with the nuts left tight. On refit the nuts of the board being refitted are loosened first.

**Reasons.**

- The panel apertures are the tightest features in the stack (0.06 mm radial at the jacks). The hardware must sit in them without stress, so the panel locates the front boards (ST-03's recommendation, accepted).
- Tight nuts before mating would freeze each front board at a random offset inside its clearance. K is one rigid board and can remove only the offset common to all of them. Loose nuts let each front board move up to its clearance (0.06 to 0.27 mm) toward K, which takes the board-level terms out of the final misalignment.
- The factory close equals the service refit, as ST-03 argued.
- K needs no precision locating pins. The pin schemes of ST-03 cost 0.35 mm on root sum of squares and add nothing a floating K needs.

**Alternatives considered.** Stack first with the panel last (the plan page): the hardware would be located by K, and the hardware-on-board terms already exceed the jack clearance (M3). Panel first with tight nuts (ST-03 scheme A as written): keeps board offsets in the pairs. K pinned to P: fails on root sum of squares at the jack boards (0.553 mm).

**Evidence status.** Aperture clearances: DERIVED from the lock and the hardware facts, with the part diameters ASSUMED as ST-03 lists them. Capture budget in this order: 0.33 to 0.36 mm on root sum of squares against 0.39 mm SOURCED; worst case 0.84 to 0.87 mm fails. **How to reverse.** The scheme is assembly procedure and hole sizes. Until ST-12 writes it into the mechanical source it is reversed by a decision row.

**Consequences.** The 777 positions mate blind in one step; inspection is by continuity, which needs daisy-chain or test access planned in ST-10. The selector row is decided in D4. ST-12 owns the pilot pins, the fixture and the oversize holes.

### EL joins P on six double-row pairs: a CLT socket on EL and a TW-SM stacker on the P tab

**What fixes 7.2 mm.** EL's rear face is at −4.6 mm and P's F.Cu at −11.8 mm. EL can move forward by at most its 0.45 mm nominal gap to the panel (front parts 1.75 mm behind a panel rear at −2.0 mm) and rearward by at most 0.4 mm (pot body fronts at −5.0 mm). P's plane is set by the toggle bushing reach: 0.78 mm of thread is left beyond the nut and washer. Neither plane has usable freedom; the 0.67 mm that a single-row pair lacks would consume every nominal clearance. The gap stays 7.2 mm.

**Decision.**

- Six pairs of 2×4 positions at the six EL sites: CLT-104-02-L-D-K-TR on EL's B.Cu (2.29 mm high) and TW-04-02-L-D-170-SM-P-TR on the P tab's F.Cu.
- Geometry: stacker body top at 4.32 mm, socket face at 7.2 − 2.29 = 4.91 mm, pin tip at 7.85 mm. Insertion is 2.94 mm against a 1.40 mm minimum. The tips pass the socket by 0.65 mm, so EL gets eight relief holes per site and the tips stand 0.25 mm proud of EL's front face, inside its 1.75 mm front height.
- Pin map per site: three signals, two AGND and three rails, each signal beside an AGND. EL gets 18 rail positions, as today.
- The P tab widens to carry the 7.57 mm double-row pattern: from y = 176 up to about y = 167.4. The cross-member at y = 165 to 167 goes (ST-03's disposition, accepted).
- The six P-to-K pass-through pairs stay single-row 1×6 at K's existing sites, on the tab's rear face.
- EL is seated and removed with the full-face platen of ST-03.
- Fallback order (plan question Q5): if the socket print does not allow the pin to pass through, or the relief holes do not fit EL, the joint becomes six short harnesses from EL to headers on the P unit, plugged on the bench. A cable is then justified by the board heights. Fallback F1 (harnesses plugged on K's front face) is withdrawn: with K closing last, a plug on K's hidden face cannot be mated.

**Reasons.** It is the only rigid pair on retained pages whose bodies fit 7.2 mm with surface-mount parts on both boards (4.32 + 2.29 = 6.61 mm). Its Z window is open upward, so the 0.4 mm board cannot be bottomed. Four positions per row is the smallest size at which both halves take a pick-and-place pad. The 2×4 map removes both pin-order remarks of ST-02.

**Alternatives considered.** Through-hole stacker in EL with an SMM socket on the tab: fits at 6.73 mm, but the 0.20 mm window is smaller than EL's gap error (0.115 mm on root sum of squares, 0.29 mm worst case) plus the socket's ±0.127 mm.
