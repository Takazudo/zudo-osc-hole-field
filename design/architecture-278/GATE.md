## Electrical acceptance and specific conflicts

A 20 mΩ connector-contact maximum cannot meet a 0.5 mΩ whole-return or1 mΩ rail-transfer limit by itself. The candidate therefore separates main power/AGND from the signal mate. A 50 mm,10×1 mm copper core strap has a 0.09975 mΩ bulk example at60 °C; a 0.10 mΩ two-joint allocation leaves0.30025 mΩ for the complete remaining return. That remainder includes shared feed, middle spreading and core copper; it is not a new budget at each board. Joint allocation is not a guaranteed component rating. Preserve 20mV hot distribution,0.20 V full-path loss and0.5 A/GH-contact limits; no equal-sharing or cancelling-rail credit.

Five simultaneous 5 V changes through existing 1 kΩ/100 nF octave receivers demand 25 mA initially in the ideal source model. The inherited 2 mA figure is a planning condition, not evidence of an actual 2 mA clamp. This operating case lies outside that condition. The proposed 900 pF direct interconnect budget does not include or excuse the switched 500 nF behind the resistors. A higher-resistance alternative is evaluated with its DC error/settling/open-selector recovery penalty rather than silently substituted. The recommended first candidate retains source parity and measures/bounds the actual five-receiver source recovery before accepting the reference interface. No changed limits or remote feedback are introduced.

Five control wipers remain unbuffered at the front, with their actual1 kΩ receiver resistors,10 MΩ pull-downs and applicable 10 nF local filters accounted separately from the remote 1 nF envelope. Implementation must satisfy the complete 300 mm length, leakage/loading and reference-error constraints. The ten refined O1/O2 wiper routes have a largest coordinate-based allocation of 295.967 mm, leaving 4.033 mm. These are routing targets, not measured or tolerance-qualified pad-to-pad maxima. The retained-bank comparison has 23 coarse conductor rows above 300 mm, including buffered signals; those rows are rejected allocations, not an accepted complete remote interface. Functional front-bank regrouping and the complete local reference-package relocation reduce that problem but leave 14 non-wiper path allocations above 300 mm (two O1, twelve O2), reaching 374.93 mm. Eight whole-card orientation cases and 19 optimistic independent pin searches do not close them; O2 at 270° improves wipers but worsens the longest allocation to 406.51 mm. Retain the unrotated candidate as the best worst-case comparison, not as an accepted complete interface. The straight-line endpoint bounds are substantially shorter, so this is a concrete circuit-aware placement/transport problem rather than proof that the architecture is impossible. Full device/state maxima, source regulation, faults and assembled joint bounds are not established by the retained worksheet allowances. #57/#59/#65/#247 remain open.

## Owner tradeoff and recommendation

The indispensable next choice is whether to authorize the isolated one-zone source/schematic/PCB stage described in NEXT-PROMPT.md, using the recommended 105 mm mixed arrangement and screw-serviced power joints. Recommend that stage because it can resolve actual routing, front-board population, joint realization and source recovery on one representative zone before replication. It does not require the owner to design supports or choose every connector parameter.

The concrete product tradeoff is service hardware versus preserving old front copper: the preferred arrangement requires new front layouts and more connector/support soldering, but gives rear core replacement without eleven rear cable disconnects or middle removal. Core-only preserves more copper and remains a comparison if old-copper reuse becomes the higher priority. Neither is adopted, priced or promised faster. A 95 mm enclosure is a compact option ; 105 mm is recommended for access margin. No further choice is needed merely to preserve this completed design checkpoint.
