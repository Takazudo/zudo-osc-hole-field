# X1 physical interconnect electrical study

The actual source-pad allocations are309.792mm for A_BUFFER,307.939mm for B_BUFFER and320.238mm for SELECTOR_REMOTE. Each traverses141mm maximum cut cable in two branches, four mated GH contacts, and three board allocations. These developed totals are not compared against a newly invented300mm developed-path limit.15 wires serve6 signal,5 rail and4 AGND conductors. All26 rail endpoints,46 AGND endpoints and5 original NC endpoints remain inventoried; six additional GH7 header NC pins remain unwired.

This candidate source study uses the complete X1 circuit and actual proposed connector pads. It is not assembled, routed or electrically qualified. Simulation, native KiCad, ERC/DRC and physical tests are NOT RUN. The existing225 oscillator crossing rows and240 return groups remain separate and unchanged.

The three signals have different obligations. A_BUFFER and B_BUFFER originate at U3305's OPA4197IPWR follower outputs. Their local unity feedback and U3304 indicator input branches stay on the jack board. The cables reach the2.2kΩ switch resistors on the control board; those downstream resistors do **not** isolate cable capacitance from the driver. SELECTOR_REMOTE originates after U3303's local follower and two499Ω resistors, and reaches U3305's precision-output receiver. The output's two499Ω resistors,100Ω sense resistor and1nF fast-feedback capacitor remain local. No ideal source or OPA4196 model is substituted for those actual amplifier dynamics.

At nominal values, a selected2.2kΩ path feeding the10MΩ switch-common pulldown loses about1.100mV at5V, before amplifier and return errors. A hypothetical momentary bridge between ideal+10V and−10V sources demands4.545mA through the two2.2kΩ resistors; this is a conditional calculation, not proof of fault tolerance or switch behavior. The998Ω SELECTOR_REMOTE network has an ideal159.47kHz pole with an assumed1nF lumped load. Neither that capacitance nor the resulting passive pole establishes actual capacitance, stability or a pass criterion.

The retained JST GH primary family contract specifies30mΩ initial and50mΩ after-test maximum contact resistance. Two mating interfaces in one front-to-middle branch therefore contribute up to60/100mΩ before additional wiring and board impedance. Preserving the earlier200µV return planning comparison would require branch/common current no greater than3.333/2mA respectively even with all other resistance zero. At10mA, those contacts alone produce0.6/1mV. Four AGND wires do not justify assuming equal sharing or dividing the resistance by four: local signal and power grounds, middle distribution, upstream supply and load paths must be solved together. The old0.5mΩ example cannot be silently carried forward or its error allocation relaxed.

Two nominal5V enable resistor networks draw approximately99.01µA, but this does not bound total rail or return current. Amplifier quiescent current, LED drive, signal-output loads, fault states and switching demand remain additional. +5V is used on the jack board only. The retained manual A/B current generator already carries planning allowances of19.8505mA/+12V,18.6505mA/−12V and0.15mA/+5V. Its sum of absolute rail allowances38.651mA would produce2.319/3.865mV across one60/100mΩ return branch, but this deliberately conservative sensitivity is not an actual simultaneous AGND current: some quiescent current closes between the positive and negative rails, and signal polarity/current redistribution matters. The source ledger explicitly omits fault and switching transients and provides no guaranteed maximum.

The family1A/AWG26 contact rating is not a guarantee for all loaded contacts, a fitted loom or elevated-temperature operation. Actual supply source placement and distribution remain open; a connector pad is not an invented upstream source.

The report distinguishes physical wire counts from endpoint/fanout and route rows. A complete signal traverses two harness branches and four mated GH interfaces, while a single branch traverses two. Return ownership includes every actual AGND endpoint and retains each bypass-to-package association. Shared capacitance is counted per actual signal, including both cables, contacts, traces and receiver loading. The global `electrical-standard.json:remote_controls` envelope applies to these buffered X1 links:300mm cable and1nF aggregate remote-net capacitance remain design targets awaiting qualification. Both each physical cable and the sum of the two cables in a complete remote signal are compared with300mm; the full developed allocation is separate. Actual capacitance remains UNKNOWN. The global2mV static precision and1mV output load-error targets also remain, alongside the precision-output source sweep of open/10k/100k and0/100pF/1nF/5nF external loads, no sustained oscillation,≤10% overshoot and1mV settling by1ms. Existing model screening does not qualify the new assembly; no external-output obligation is removed.

Next electrical closure requires actual aggregate capacitance, driver/output impedances and full return/current distributions, reviewed transfer/error allocations, and manufacturer-conditioned active behavior. A bounded later characterization should exercise both selected inputs, overlapping/open switching, opposing levels, actual loads and LED activity, and compare DC error, transfer, settling, ringing and return shift against the captured circuit. That specification is a proposal, not executed bench evidence. Precise constraints may support the next bounded function study, but no other function is implemented here.

The generated report includes ten selected actual-pad local feedback/indicator branch comparisons against Stage6, plus the complete endpoint inventory. Local geometry tradeoffs remain visible; improved output-feedback proximity does not imply all LED feedback loops improved. Board trace allocations use1.15×octile XY+7mm and explicit face-to-face Z separation where applicable. They are reproducible engineering allowances, not routed copper or measured loop performance.

Foreign-tail substrate apertures, mount holes and component courtyards may require additional trace detours. The octile factor and7mm extra are allowances, not obstacle-aware routed solutions; no complete allocation is evidence that a legal copper path fits the proposed substrate.
