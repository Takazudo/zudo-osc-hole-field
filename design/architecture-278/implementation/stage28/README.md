## Blocking finding: the Stage10 DIN labels are mirrored against the HARTING views

Both drawings (re-downloaded 2026-10-17, bytes identical to the Stage5 receipts) show
the board drillings as seen **from the connector body toward the board**. The female
arrow A comes from above the body with the tails pointing down. The male arrow B comes
from below the body with the tails pointing up. The locked frame is +x right and +y down,
seen from the panel front. The Stage10 board planes put z toward the rear: the female body
sits behind A01-middle (z=50) and the male body in front of A01-core (z=69.2).

So the female drawing must map to the board with determinant +1 and the male with −1.
The 96 Stage10 labeled positions fit exact rigid maps with determinants **−1** (female)
and **+1** (male). Both are mirrored. They are mirrored consistently, so female and male
label p still coincide, and a mated pair would join equal nets through physically
different contacts. Contact-numbered inspection, pin-1 marking and male orientation
would not match the parts. Stage5 had already recorded the "exact mirrored viewing
direction" as UNRESOLVED; Stage24 read the views but left the installed transform null.
The root cause is the Stage6 registration. Its asserted matrices act on a paper frame
(u right, v down, v=−Y), and Stage10 copies them as `signed_transform`. Their
determinants are not drawing-to-global determinants. The mated pair stays electrically
consistent only because every 09031966922 position is the standard pin type A, with no
preleading or lagging pins.

If the 0.3 mm fixing-hole offset is toward row a, as Stage22 recorded, the only
proper-handed relabeling that keeps the fixed 96-hole grid **and** the Stage10 female
fixing holes is column reversal: the physical contact (row, n) lands at the Stage10
position labeled (row, 33−n). Row reversal would move those holes 0.6 mm. The offset side could not be re-read from
the raster in this session. If it is toward row c, row reversal becomes the
hole-preserving choice and the Stage10 fixing holes are misplaced as well. M-01 and Q-M04
record the side. The
candidate is in `report.json → din.candidates`. It is **not adopted**. Q-M01, M-01 and
owner decision Q-O01 resolve it. Until then, D02 stays `CONFLICT_REQUIRES_DECISION` and no
board copper may bind DIN labels to XY. The generator ties D02 to the computed result.

## DIN decision summary

- **Hole size (D03, D14):** the drawing calls for `96x Ø1±0,1` "board drillings".
  Finished plated 0.90–1.10 is still the Stage24/25 *proposal*, not a manufacturer
  statement. JLC's tighter option (retrieved 10:51Z) is published as "Press-fit holes
  ±0.05 mm", for finished 0.55–2.0 mm, multilayer ENIG and circular holes only. Whether it
  applies to soldered tails is UNCONFIRMED.
- **Position (D04):** `⊕ Ø0,05` has no datum letters. It is a pattern tolerance against the
  boxed 2.54 grid; datum A is the contact pattern and only controls fixing-hole symmetry.
  Ø0.05 means 0.025 mm radial. JLC's ±0.075 per axis can reach 0.106 mm radial, so the
  published default does not guarantee it. That does not prove every route fails.
- **Fixing holes (D05):** 2× Ø2.8 +0.1/−0, 90 ±0.1, symmetry 0.1 to A, offset 0.3 ±0.1
  from row b. Plating is not stated, the offset side could not be re-read from the raster
  in this session, and JLC publishes no round-NPTH tolerance. The NPTH plan stays an
  unadopted candidate.
- **Male (D06):** board drillings show only the 96 holes; the Ø2.55 flange holes are body
  features. No board holes are invented. How the male is retained against service load
  is UNCONFIRMED (Q-M05), because solder joints are not credited as the anchor.
- **Thickness, seating, forces, fixture (D07–D10):** no board thickness on either drawing.
  Tails are 2.9 +0.15/−0.05 (female) and 3 +0.4 (male). Stage10's 19.2 mm separation lies
  inside the retained catalogue window of 18.3–20 mm. Forces and cycles are not retained.
  All of these are UNDECIDED or UNCONFIRMED.
- **Process, cleaning, inspection (D11–D13):** JLC lists mixed SMD+THT with THT by wave
  soldering. Exact-part temperature/time limits, cleaning compatibility, acceptance class
  and records are UNCONFIRMED.

## Harness scope and candidate

In scope is the A01 representative transport contract (Stage15 interfaces = Stage16
`transport_contract`):

- 4 JST GH cables (J-SIG 7, J-PWR 4, C-SIG 6, K-PWR 3; 20 conductors, 1:1 straight)
- 14 permanent tails (8 LED and 6 jack S/T)
- the DIN pair J17909/J17910 (96 contacts: 16 assigned, 80 NC)
- 22 non-BOM land annotations, whose flags are kept

Roles: POWER 5, RETURN 8, REFERENCE 4, SIGNAL 9, INDICATOR 8. The exclusions (upstream
supply, board copper, A02–A06, the O1–O5 ledgers whose mating face is OPEN, fixed device
bodies, chassis earth) are listed in the ledger.

- **Wire:** the JST GH catalogue allows AWG #30–#26 and insulation OD 0.76–1.0 mm, rated
  1.0 A only at AWG26, 50 V, −40…105 °C. Candidate is AWG26 for every GH conductor because
  Stage10 keeps a 0.5 A contact limit. Tail wire OD must be strictly below the Stage10
  guide minimum opening of 1.0 mm. The margin is `null` until the vendor states it
  (Q-H03). No wire
  MPN is selected.
- **Termination:** the vendor crimps and solders. The owner does no soldering.
- **Lengths:** Stage10 nominal cuts (90/75/115/85 ±3) are proposals. Each tail's 85 mm is
  about four times its 17–20 mm straight distance, and its basis is not documented.
  Measured path, cut length, bend radius and anchor position are `null`, with M-09 as the
  method.
- **Proxy screen:** four J-PWR straight proxies cross the J-SIG middle release sweep.
  This is routing input, not a defect verdict.
- **Assembly drawing candidate:** shows the core withdrawal (40 mm, K-PWR disconnected
  first), handles, mount tool access and release sweeps. A01 has no rear trimmers. The six
  O1 rear trimmers (6 mm tool cylinders) are recorded as a keep-out rule for any future O1
  harness.

## Route and next decisions

First route (R-1…R-4 in `harness-policy.json`):

1. JLC multilayer ENIG boards with the ±0.05 option, if confirmed for soldered holes.
2. Written Ø0.05 position and 2.8 mm fixing-hole capability, plus a first-article hole
   report.
3. Assembler wave or selective soldering of the exact HARTING parts in a seating fixture.
4. Vendor-built, labeled and 100 %-tested harnesses, cut only after an unpowered fit-up
   measurement.

A precision fabricator (ALT-1) is the comparison if Q-F01–Q-F03 fail. An adapter board,
a different connector or connectorized tails (ALT-2, ALT-3) remain separate design
changes and are not adopted.
