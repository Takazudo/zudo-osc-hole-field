# O1 interface contract and replication mapping — issue #280

This is a source-backed candidate ownership contract, not an adopted architecture or a qualified circuit. `interface-contract.json` is generated by `python3 scripts/architecture278/extract.py`; `--check` compares every byte. Input hashes include compiler Python, electrical standard, symbols, partition, source I/O report and locked placements. The parent work note identifies the immutable repository baseline. No parked CAD is adopted.

## Ownership and complete endpoint accounting

The machine packet includes O1 and the shared OCTAVE_REF assembly, every source unit, package, Ref-pin and net, explicit NC pins, source board, panel UID, exact identity where present, fitted/DNP flags and decoupling ownership. `instance_mapping` translates each O1 unit and pin to O2 and O5 using the real designator function; local net prefixes change, global service polarity does not. Panel references are taken from the locked source bindings, never calculated from an assumed stride.

| Source region | Preferred candidate | Limited-change alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jack, JL/JR | Independently supported front jack PCB | Existing JL/JR retained |
| control, P | Independently supported front control PCB | Existing P retained |
| selector, O1 | Fixed octave switch adapter | Existing octave adapter retained |
| core, K | Removable O1 rear core card | Removable O1 rear core card |
| shared OCTAVE_REF on K | Supported shared reference zone, not replicated per card | Shared reference on retained K |

The current `io_partition.refine` already packs channels by region and rebuilds local decoupling. This contract preserves those entire packages and their spare/power units. There is no unexplained cross-card package split in this mapping. Moving a receiver buffer to a pot board is **not** included: that would require a new channel/package partition, decoupling, source parity and qualification. Separate physical front boards remain redesign work even when electrical region assignments can be retained.

Raw input fault switches and high impedance receivers stay on the jack interface for 1V/FM/PWM/SYNC. SIN/TRI/SAW/PUL output drivers, protection and feedback stay there too. Their existing scaled core signals may cross the middle zone with a paired return; raw oscillator waveforms may not. Timing capacitor, exponential summing, scale/compensation/high-frequency nodes, VEE5 and its feedback remain core-local. Shared references may cross as explicitly accounted services; this does not authorize a general audio/CV bus, hidden normal or remote feedback sensing.

## Eight controls and six rear adjustments

| Control | Actual path | Nominal source resistance bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FM±, PWM± | Raw 10 kΩ pot wiper → existing high impedance core receiver | Rpot/4 = 2.5 kΩ plus source/contact terms |
| TUNE, FINE, PW | Raw 100 kΩ pot wiper → existing high impedance core receiver | Rpot/4 = 25 kΩ plus source/contact terms |
| OCT | Shared buffered tap → mechanical selector → core filter/receiver | Tap driver, switch, series/filter network; worst-case bound open |
| VCO/LFO | Local LFO reference/AGND → mechanical switch → range resistor | Source/contact and pull-down network; worst-case bound open |
| SYNC | Conditioned SYNC_GATE → switch → HARD/SOFT edge network | Source/contact and edge network; worst-case bound open |

The raw wipers are **not buffered at the control board**. The nominal Rpot/4 values omit pot tolerance, upstream impedance, receiver bias/leakage and frequency dependence. They are not accepted electrical error budgets. All exact endpoints and high/low polarity are retained in JSON. Maximum remote envelope remains 300 mm / 1 nF including connector, cable, trace and probe; shared octave 1 nF is aggregate across all five branches, not per branch. Worst-case loading, settling, leakage and error allocations remain blockers.

Six O1 trims are RVO1106 BASE, RVO1107 RZ, RVO1108 SCALE, RVO1109 HF, RVO1110 SINE_SYMMETRY and RVO1111 SINE_LEVEL. They remain rear-core local. HF's 20 kΩ exact MPN is absent in source and remains unresolved. Shared octave span belongs to OCTAVE_REF and is inventoried as part of that complete assembly, not a seventh O1 trimmer. Tool/probe access and powered calibration safety are separate mechanical/bench gates.

## Harness and contact accounting

The 11 original harnesses are JL-K-15/16, K-O1-1/2 and K-P-81…87. Their 51 fitted conductors comprise signals and explicit AGND returns. Both original connector faces, original exact GH headers/housings/contacts, manufacturer pin maps, direction, current bounds and proposal/status fields are retained. This is 22 original headers and 102 contact/crimp terminations; it is not the final candidate bill.

Preferred candidate replaces each centre span with middle-zone traces and two short keyed flexible links where endpoints remain on different boards. A literal one-for-one implementation would require four mating locations and four crimp terminations per original conductor (204 crimps for these 51 conductors), versus two originally. This is an illustrative topology count, **not a specified final pinout**: consolidation, extra returns, power/reference distribution and service fixtures must be designed and counted before any claimed reduction. Core-only retains front routing and changes the core-side disconnect; final connector counts likewise remain open.

The 13 starting service classes are +12V, -12V, +5V, AGND, OSC_REF5, OSC_REFN5, OSC_GATE_REF and OSC_OCT0…5. These are not a 13-pin connector. Returns and parallel contacts must follow actual current/error budgets; no equal sharing is assumed. Candidate connectors require unique polarizing/keying, visible pin-1/mating orientation and prevention of cross-family misconnection. Exact MPNs, seating geometry and physical pin allocation remain an owner-reviewed design choice. Existing GH identities are historical mapping inputs, not automatic new-architecture selections.

## Existing rear terminals before additions

The packet preserves **all 36 existing load-side factory terminals** across the instrument, with exact source reference, net/pin, board, coordinates, side, custom solder-land identity and unresolved process status. Keeping the whole list avoids silently treating shared supply terminals as dedicated O1 I/O. It separately preserves the existing K-side faces of the 11 O1 harnesses. Their source function, protection gaps, load/support path and service requirements are recorded; solder lands and connector friction cannot carry structural loads.

These factory solder terminals are not new user performance I/O. The source does not establish that every listed terminal is rear-accessible in a completed enclosure. Converting a factory-wired K terminal to a removable card interface is therefore a concrete unresolved design task, not a claimed solder-free replacement solution. No extra performance outputs, duplicate jacks or new test terminals are authorized. Test adapters must have a separate fixture bill and cannot be counted as final instrument connections.

## Fault exposure and evidence limits

Only power-off, discharged removal is proposed; no hot swap. Existing partial-power/off-state injection, missing rail, ramp/brownout, reverse/misconnection and output short/recovery behavior remains unqualified under #59/#247. Continuous AGND does not eliminate remote ground error. Return/protection rationale in each net record identifies the preserved topology and its open fault qualification, rather than asserting generic buffered safety.

Thirteen source/mutation tests exercise missing/duplicate endpoints, symbol pin removal, whole-package integrity, connector polarity, prohibited sensitive crossing and O1→O2/O5 mapping. They prove bookkeeping on the captured source, not ERC/DRC, electrical suitability or bench performance. Independent circuit review must assess these exact final inputs. The unresolved connector assignment, worst-case impedance/loading and support requirements prevent claiming the full physical interface contract complete at the decision gate; the recommendation must retain these blockers.
